## Deferred (explicitly not now)

- Accounts, profiles, cross-room leaderboards.
- Native app or store distribution — a **non-goal**, not a deferral.
- Server-side persistence of any kind.
- Spectator streaming / big-screen companion view.
- Monetisation.

| 2026-08-04 | **A client owns its own position; the server owns every catch.** Nobody can simulate a finger, so Cat and Mouse cannot be refereed the way a goat's arc is. Truncated rather than rejected — a slow link produces big steps through no fault of the player, and refusing them punishes whoever is already suffering. What that does **not** stop is a client reporting itself a few pixels behind where it really dragged, and that is on the record rather than implied | [specs/games/cat-and-mouse.md](specs/games/cat-and-mouse.md) §9 |
| 2026-08-04 | **Your own icon never waits for a round trip; everyone else is deliberately ~90 ms late.** Two different jobs in one file. Everyone else is *interpolated between the last two ticks* rather than extrapolated forward: guessing ahead from a velocity puts an icon where its player never went and then yanks it back, which in a chase reads as teleporting. The cost is that a catch can look slightly late to a bystander, and nothing is scored on what a bystander sees | [../www/src/games/cat-and-mouse/game.ts](../www/src/games/cat-and-mouse/game.ts) |

| 2026-08-04 | **Sling Puck's tap fallback is removed: a release fires only if the puck was pulled.** §14 asked whether tap-to-launch was "too good"; it was. Rack pucks rest inside the band's zone, so even a bare grab-and-release counted as a stretch — the pulled-or-not rule closes both. The cost is named rather than hidden: anyone who cannot drag cannot play it, and a replacement fallback has to be slower and less accurate than a drag or it becomes optimal again | [specs/games/sling-puck.md](specs/games/sling-puck.md) §7, §13 |
| 2026-08-04 | **A puck is carried to the sling, never teleported to it.** A grab records the finger-to-centre offset and keeps it, so the puck stays where it lies and only moving your finger moves it; any puck can be grabbed, moving or not, because one rattling back down your half is the one you most want to reload. | 2026-08-04 | **The armed target drifts, and the drift is arithmetic rather than random.** A target that is visible *and* still can be covered by a thumb before the signal, which gives back the accuracy the target was added for.
